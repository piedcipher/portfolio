# Navratri

Today is Dussehra. Vijayadashami.

Whenever Navratri comes, first thing that comes to mind is my father.

In the 80s and 90s, he used to organize garba in our society. Back then it felt huge. Full lanes, heavy crowd, proper noise, proper energy. It felt like the whole area was out.

Then it stopped for years.

Around 2010, garba started again because of the Vadigam brothers. That time also had strong vibe. Orchestra scene every day. I played every single day.

Same phase, one more memory — I got my first laptop, a Lenovo, as a birthday gift from a relative. Still remember that feeling.

After 2013, I stopped going for garba. Society or outside, almost nothing.

Outside garba I had gone only once before that — in 2005 with cousins.

Then in 2025, went once with a close friend at Adani Shantigram. Didn’t play garba, just roamed around.

Now I mostly like listening to garba more than playing it.

Garba plans usually happen somewhere around me, not with me.

I can still go alone — I already do that for restaurants, movies, stand-up comedy, long walks, all of it.  
But garba feels different.

The sound still hits hard. Dhol, vocals, crowd in the background — everything takes me back.

Maybe my inner child is still searching for those days.

It’s been 7 years since dad passed away.

Some festivals don’t feel like events.  
They feel like memories returning.

Navratri is like that for me.  
Every time.
