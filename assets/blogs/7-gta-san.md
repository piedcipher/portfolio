# GTA: San Andreas

I bought it for Android in 2017.

By then I had already left B.Tech midway and later finished it from 2017 to 2020 at a local college in Ahmedabad. I was still recovering from jaundice, and things at home were not easy. It was one of those times when even small things mattered more than they should.

Before that, I had played it around 2008 or 2009 on a computer with a neighbour, and then again on my laptop in 2010. It had been sitting in my head for years before I bought it again.

I’m replaying it in 2026 and at 90% completion right now.

- Vigilante missions give full armour.
- Paramedic jobs refill your health.
- Taxi work gives nitro and hydraulics in all cabs.
- Fire brigade missions give fireproof ability.
- Export car side missions are one of the best money makers.
- A few courier missions unlock property income that actually starts paying off.

That is what makes San Andreas feel bigger than most open-world games. It never really stops asking you to do a little more. One job leads to another, and before long the city starts feeling like yours.

I still think about how good the world feels when you move through it. The characters, the vibe, the little details — it still holds up. Even with older mechanics, it has a mood that is hard to fake.

And then there is the radio. Queen of Hearts, Hollywood Swinging, and I Love Rainy Nights somehow made the whole city feel more alive. They fit a certain kind of late-night drive perfectly.

There is something strange about returning to a game you have known across different parts of your life. The old memories are still there — the neighbour’s PC, the laptop, the Android version in 2017, and the replay in 2026. It feels like a thread that runs through all of it.

San Andreas has always been more than just a GTA game for me. It is one of those games that stays with you.

> "Ain't no place like home, but this is close enough."
